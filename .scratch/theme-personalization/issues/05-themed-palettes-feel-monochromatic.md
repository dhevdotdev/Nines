# Themed palettes feel monochromatic in the live app

Category: enhancement
Status: ready-for-agent

## Reported problem

After selecting one of the named palettes in Appearance, the app feels like it
has been tinted with one color rather than given a complete palette. The
screens lack a convincing splash of coordinated colors, so the result feels
lifeless despite the theme being described as a Material/HCT-derived scheme.

## Desired direction

Each named theme should produce a visually intentional, harmonious palette:
primary, secondary, tertiary, container, surface, and supporting roles should
have enough tonal and chromatic distinction to create hierarchy while still
reading as one theme. The result must remain accessible and use proper Material
role pairings rather than raw decorative colors.

## Scope to clarify during triage

- Verify whether the issue is in palette generation, role consumption by the
  app surfaces, or both.
- Compare the resolved `ColorScheme` roles and the rendered app for several
  named themes, including Catppuccin and Tokyo Night.
- Decide whether the fix belongs in the shared HCT palette configuration, the
  app's Material role usage, or both.

## Constraints

- Preserve Wallpaper dynamic color and Custom seed behavior unless verification
  shows they share the same defect.
- Preserve readable light/dark contrast and Material paired roles.
- Do not replace the shared root theme with per-screen hardcoded colors.
- Keep per-Habit identity colors distinct from the app palette.

## Agent Brief

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Make named theme palettes visibly expressive instead of
monochromatic while preserving Material role semantics and accessibility.

**Current behavior:**

Named sources currently behave like one accent seed passed through a shared
HCT/Material generator. The app receives a complete `ColorScheme`, but the
rendered result often reads as one dominant tint because supporting roles and
expressive surfaces do not create enough visible variation.

**Desired behavior:**

Named themes use coordinated palette recipes with an anchor, companion, and
highlight character. Nines derives accessible light/dark Material roles from
those recipes and uses the roles intentionally in expressive surfaces. The
Appearance preview becomes a small composition showing the actual scheme:
surface/container, primary, secondary, tertiary, and paired foreground roles.

Wallpaper and Custom use the same resolution contract. Custom remains a
single-seed editor for now. Named sources remain “inspired by” themes, not
literal ports or copied role tables.

**Key interfaces:**

- `AppColorSource` — named sources need richer palette identity than a single
  undifferentiated seed, without changing persisted source selection semantics.
- The public root color-resolution seam — must resolve every source to one
  accessible light/dark `ColorScheme`.
- Appearance preview components — must render role relationships from the
  resolved scheme rather than decorative raw swatches.
- Existing Material color-role consumers — audit and make targeted changes so
  primary, secondary, and tertiary expression appears in previews, onboarding
  artwork, selected controls, progress visuals, and supporting containers.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- [ ] Named themes have coordinated anchor/companion/highlight palette
      recipes, including Catppuccin, Tokyo Night, Dracula, and the remaining
      theme collection sources.
- [ ] Catppuccin, Tokyo Night, Dracula, Custom, and Wallpaper are verified in
      both light and dark mode; their primary, secondary, and tertiary roles
      are measurably distinct without sacrificing paired foreground contrast.
- [ ] Appearance previews show a truthful mini composition using actual
      surface, container, primary, secondary, tertiary, and `on*` roles.
- [ ] The live app visibly uses the supporting roles in targeted expressive
      surfaces without recoloring every reading surface or introducing
      per-screen hardcoded theme colors.
- [ ] Wallpaper dynamic color, Custom persistence, system light/dark mode,
      per-Habit identity colors, and named-theme “inspired by” boundaries
      remain intact.
- [ ] Focused automated tests cover palette resolution, role distinction,
      contrast-safe pairings, and persistence; connected/device review confirms
      the result feels visibly richer in the selected themes.

**Out of scope:**

- A full screen-by-screen visual redesign.
- A multi-color Custom editor; Custom remains one seed for this effort.
- Literal copying of external theme implementations, assets, or raw role
  tables.
- Manual light/dark mode selection or changes to per-Habit palette ownership.
